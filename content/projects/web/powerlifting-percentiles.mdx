Where your lifts sit among competitive powerlifters, and how far that answer moves when you change who you are compared against. Live at [plpercentiles.netlify.app](https://plpercentiles.netlify.app).

Built on the [OpenPowerlifting](https://openpowerlifting.gitlab.io/opl-csv/bulk-csv.html) bulk export — 4.0M meet results, 2.65M valid full-power performances, 774k distinct lifters.

## Why

Two calculators can score the same lifts twenty percentile points apart and both be correct. The population is doing the work, and it is usually invisible: 2025 or all-time, tested or any meet, entries or people, raw or everything.

So the comparison is the interface. Enter your lifts once and they are scored against every cohort simultaneously; the spread between them is the headline, not any single row.

## What profiling the source turned up

Filter design came out of the data rather than assumption. Four things materially change the answer:

- **High-school federations are 28% of valid full-power results**, and their meets permit squat suits — so they land in `Single-ply` and `Unlimited`, not `Raw`. THSPA alone is the largest federation in the dataset. Median squat in `Unlimited` swings 390 → 700 lb once school meets are removed; `Single-ply` swings 420 → 474 lb. Any equipment dropdown that ignores this is describing teenagers.
- **211,198 rows are one lifter entered twice.** 96% of duplicate groups differ only by `Division` — the same person in Open *and* their age class. Counting entries weights frequent competitors, who are stronger, several times over.
- **`Tested` only ever takes the value `Yes`**, with blanks for everything else, and it is set per meet. Federations are internally mixed: ~37% of USPA meets, ~41% of WRPF, ~0.4% of USPF.
- **`AgeClass` covers 73% against `Age` at 63%**, and missingness tracks federation record-keeping rather than age. Lifters with no recorded age are ~44 lb weaker at the median squat, so filtering on age quietly filters on paperwork. Opt-in, never default.

Also: the sport is getting stronger fast, and half of it is composition. Median raw squat runs 413 lb all-time, 424 lb for 2020+, 441 lb in 2025. First-time competitors alone went 380 → 397 lb between 2015 and 2025, while their share of entries fell from 47% to 31%.

## Data pipeline

`build_site_data.py` emits one columnar binary per era at **12 bytes per lifter**:

```
flags:u16   sex | equipment | tested | school | ageClass
geo:u8      country
fed:u8      sanctioning body
bw:u16      hectograms
sq,bp,dl:u16
```

2020+ is 388k lifters, 4.7 MB raw and 2.1 MB gzipped. Era is a file rather than a filter because it changes the de-duplication basis — "one row per lifter" only means something relative to a window, and restricting years *after* de-duplicating would keep only those whose best-ever total happened to fall inside it.

## Percentiles

The browser decodes columns into typed arrays and computes everything itself, so no filter combination is precomputed and every one responds instantly.

Values are hectograms in a `Uint16Array`, so the range is small enough that a counting histogram at full 0.1 kg resolution costs ~24 KB — and doubles as a counting sort. Percentiles and quantiles are therefore **exact order statistics**, not interpolation off a fitted normal.

Ties matter here. 8,716 raw men bench exactly 115 kg, so the percentile at that weight genuinely spans 38.5 to 42.1. The reported value is the midpoint of the step.

The same clustering bit the charts: with arbitrary bin widths the histograms alias against round loadings and render as a comb of alternating tall and short bars. Bin edges snap to multiples of the 2.5 kg plate step.

## Filters

Global, faceted, each showing how many lifters it leaves — sex, equipment, drug-tested, age class, bodyweight range, federation group, country, school meets. Nothing typed leaves the browser.

## Stack

- Vite 6, React 19, TypeScript, Tailwind v4
- No chart library — SVG, drawn from the counting histograms
- Python + pandas for the offline pipeline

## Deploy

- Netlify, auto-build on push to `main`
- Binaries served from `public/data` with long-lived immutable caching
