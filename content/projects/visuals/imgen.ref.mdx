# Aesthetic reference  

Bullet skeleton. Add / cut / annotate freely; `prompts.md` reads from this.  

## Music context  
- Noise / breakcore. 220+ BPM, often higher.  
- Hardcore kicks; distorted reese basses; complex, unrelenting breaks.  
- Dissonant, intentionally offensive, abrasive.  
- Visual pacing should match: micro-cuts, strobes, twitch, mechanical jitter, the rare calm beats contain disturbing biotechnological imagery.  

## Direct references  
- Chris Cunningham, *Rubber Johnny* (Aphex Twin, 2005). IR-green, contorted body, single light source, black void, dirty and dark basement. *Monkey Drummer* (Aphex Twin, 2001). Multi-armed mechanical primate hybrid playing drums in a sterile white observation cell, single subject framed centered, white void background, mechanical-organic creature performing a precise wrong-but-rhythmic action, fast articulated limbs. 
- Autechre, *Second Bad Vibel* video; scientific, alien biotech, purposefully vague, distorted/glitching.
- *Alien* (1979). Giger biomechanical. Wet, ribbed, organic-into-metal, fractal/heavily repeated and complex elements, CRT and industrial tech.   

## Related self-projects (style anchors)  
- NecroType (death-metal logo generator: glyph → distortion → multi-pass directional extrusion). Spiky, encrusted, illegible-on-purpose. Black/death metal logos, like a sharp, toxic rorschac.  
- Personal site: Hard lines and text on pure black backgrounds. Glitch animations, purposefully elusive/inaccessible. Simple and navigable to people who pay attention, scary to those who don't.
- Dotfiles: My Arch/i3 build. Sme aesthetics as site, pure black with sparse hard red lines. Zero trackpad/mouse needed IF you know/understand the commands. Look comes from functional design instead of purposeful aestheticism.

## Visual elements / motifs  
- Exposed circuitry, PCBs, ribbon cables, breadboards mid-assembly.  
- CRT screens (curved glass, scanlines, phosphor glow), oscilloscope traces, monochrome HUDs, broken UIs.  
- Tactile machine interfaces: toggle switches, hex screws, brass contacts, patch cables.  
- Biological intrusions: tendons, ribs, viscera, capillaries, wet membranes growing through housings, eyes, breathing/morphing/contorting.  
- Insect/arthropod morphology: chitin, mandibles, segmented limbs.  
- Disassembled / mid-autopsy machinery.  

## Materials & textures  
- Wet chrome, oxidized brass, scratched aluminium.  
- Black latex, raw flesh, exposed bone, gristle.  
- Condensation, oily film, slime trails, blood beading.  
- Dust, lint, hair caught in mechanisms.  
- Phosphor decay, CRT bloom, video tape dropouts, chromatic fringe.  

## Color & light  
- Mostly black. Single hard light source. Strong falloff.  
- Sterile fluorescent white-green (lab tile), sodium-orange (CRT phosphor / corridor sodium), sickly amber, bio-red (blood-on-monitor), desaturated clinical green, IR night-vision green, sickly cyan.  
- High contrast, crushed shadows, blown specular hits.  
- No daylight, no warmth, no skin tones.  

## Capture medium / quality  
- **Shitty old digital camera**. Cheap consumer-grade DV/DVR, early-2000s camcorders, late-90s digital point-and-shoot, low-end phone cams. Not "film grain", not "cinematic film stock".  
- Heavy digital noise, banded gradients, blown-out highlights, crushed blacks, chromatic fringing, dirty lens.  
- Aggressive auto-exposure pumping; autofocus hunting; harsh on-board flash or single flashlight as sole light source.  
- VHS / VHS-C / Hi8 tape: scanlines, dropouts, head-switch noise at bottom, magnetic distortion, chroma bleed.  
- Webcam / cheap surveillance: low resolution, low frame rate, jpeg block artifacts, IR-cut filter switching, timestamps burned in.  
- Found-footage / handheld POV: shaky cam, vertical-ish framing, wobble, accidental occlusions by fingers.  
- Never "RED camera", never "anamorphic", never "ARRI", never "cinema lens". Anti-cinema is the rule.  

## Camera & movement  
- Macro shots, extreme close-ups (mm-scale).  
- Slow mechanical push-ins or handheld jitter; never smooth gimbal aesthetics.  
- Probe-lens look: stuck inside the machine.  
- Spasmodic subject motion; mechanical seizing; insectile twitch.  

## Mood  
- Inaccessible, hostile, clinical.  
- Wrong-functioning rather than dead. Things moving when they shouldn't.  
- Claustrophobic, low-ceiling, no exits visible.  

## Tone / theme  
- **Darker, confusing, disorienting**. Not action-horror; existential / clinical / unwell.  
- Lab-leak / analog-horror fictional frame: **classified surveillance footage of biological-mechanical experiments gone wrong**. Loose conceit: footage from an unnamed underground facility, mid-90s vintage, recovered after the event.  
- The viewer should not always be sure WHAT they're looking at. Scale ambiguity (macro or room?), partial occlusions, wrong proportions, near-symmetry that's uncannily off, mirrored duplications that don't match, subjects morphing between forms, light from impossibly many directions at once.  
- Slow observational pace punctuated by violent micro-spasms on a single region while the rest holds still. Not chaotic everything-moving energy; precise, isolated wrongness.  
- In-frame surveillance furniture: timestamp burn-ins (`19960714 23:47`), `REC ●` indicators, `LAB 7 ████████`, redacted black bars, tracking numbers in corners, biohazard signage on doors/cells. These are SUBJECT, not post-process.  

## Specific subject vocabulary  
- specimen / test subject / containment / observation chamber / observation glass / chimera / hybrid / mutation / outbreak / restraint / IV line / sterile tile / fluorescent ballast / biohazard / quarantine / petri / micrograph  
- pulsing membrane / exposed sinew / wet tissue / chitinous nodes / glass tank / wet smear / capillaries / nodule / cyst / tumour / fissure  
- analog horror / VHS broadcast intrusion / EAS interruption / NTSC ringing / signal degradation (as on-screen subject of a CRT, not as a post effect on the gen)  

## Subject (no identifiable people)  
- No recognizable human figures, faces, or bodies as subject. Prompts should describe **abstract biomechanical masses**, **writhing amalgams of flesh and metal**, **disembodied viscera**, **appendage-like tendrils**, etc.  
- Anatomical fragments OK if context-stripped (a single eye-like aperture, a fragment of ribcage embedded in brass, tendons stretched over machinery). They should not read as "a person".  
- Things glimpsed in scenes: specimens, growths, biomechanical entities, NOT people.  
- POV is fine (the viewer's eye is the camera) but the camera should not look down to see "their" hands; if appendages appear at frame edge they should be ambiguous and abstract.  

## Avoid  
- Cinematic warmth, golden hour, lens flare.  
- Cleanly rendered AI sheen, soft-skin portraits, generic cyberpunk neon.  
- Daylight, exterior wide shots, nature, calm pacing.  
- Anything that reads as marketing / "concept art".
