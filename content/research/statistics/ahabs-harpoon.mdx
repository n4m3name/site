Replicability is under sustained scrutiny: only 11% of landmark preclinical cancer findings replicate, and roughly 90% of drug-development trials fail for lack of efficacy. We ask a concrete question: given a corpus of historical results, each reporting a $p$-value $p$, sample size $n$, and test dimension $q$, can we flag the subset of significant findings that will fail to replicate?

## 1. Theory

### The eJAB approximation to the Bayes factor

Velidi et al. (2025) give a generalized Jeffreys's approximate objective Bayes factor in closed form, needing only $(p, n, q)$:

$$
\mathrm{eJAB}_{01}(p,n,q) = \sqrt{n}\,\exp\!\left\{ -\tfrac{1}{2}\,\frac{n^{1/q}-1}{n^{1/q}}\,Q_{\chi^{2}_{q}}(1-p) \right\},
$$

with $Q_{\chi^2_q}$ the chi-squared quantile function. It is a closed-form approximation, in $(p, n, q)$ alone, to the Bayes factor for $\mathcal{H}_0$ against $\mathcal{H}_1$ under a unit-information prior: no priors to elicit, no numerical integration, no MCMC. $\mathrm{eJAB}_{01} > 1$ is Bayesian support for the null. Corpus-scale screening of historical $p$-values is feasible precisely because the approximation is this cheap.

A **Bayes/NHST contradiction** is a result significant at level $\alpha$ ($p \leq \alpha$) that nonetheless earns Bayesian support for the null ($\mathrm{eJAB}_{01} > C$). We call them candidates for non-replication. Velidi et al. fix $C = 1$; this choice is not optimal, so we treat $C$ as a tunable parameter and *calibrate $\hat C^{*}$ from the corpus itself*.

### Derivation 1: the contradiction cutoff

The eJAB map is strictly monotone in $p$, so the contradiction condition inverts to a lower $p$-value cutoff dependent on sample size $n$ and test dimension $q$. Abbreviate:

$$
g(n,q,C) = \frac{2\,n^{1/q}}{n^{1/q}-1}\,\log\frac{\sqrt n}{C}.
$$

Taking logarithms of $\mathrm{eJAB}_{01} > C$, then applying the chi-squared CDF $F_{\chi^2_q}$:

$$
\mathrm{eJAB}_{01} > C \;\Longleftrightarrow\; Q_{\chi^2_q}(1-p) < g(n,q,C) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; p > 1 - F_{\chi^2_q}\!\big(g(n,q,C)\big).
$$

Defining $f(n,q,C) = 1 - F_{\chi^2_q}(g)$, a result is a contradiction **iff** $p \in (f, \alpha]$: the threshold $C$ acts as a per-test lower $p$-value cutoff $f$. Crucially, $g$ (and hence $f$) depends on both $n$ and $q$, so a single $C$ induces a *different* $p$-value cutoff for every test in the corpus.

### Derivation 2: calibrating Ĉ*

> **Key assumption (left-tail uniformity).** A true-null $p$-value, conditioned on $p \leq u_p$, is uniform: $\;p \mid p \leq u_p, \mathcal{H}_0 \sim \mathrm{Unif}(0, u_p)$.

A true-null window study is then significant at level $\alpha$ with probability exactly $\alpha / u_p$. The empirical contradiction rate

$$
\hat{\mathbb{P}}(\alpha, C) = \frac{1}{N}\!\!\sum_{i:\,p_i \leq u_p}\!\! \mathbf{1}\!\left[p_i \leq \alpha,\ \mathrm{eJAB}_{01,i} > C\right]
$$

is a step function in $\alpha$. We pick $\hat C^{*}$ so it tracks the target $\alpha / u_p$ in integrated squared error:

$$
\hat C^{*} = \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{C \geq 0} \int_0^{u_p}\!\big(\hat{\mathbb{P}}(\alpha, C) - \alpha/u_p\big)^2 \, d\alpha.
$$

Since $\hat{\mathbb{P}}$ is piecewise constant, the integral collapses to a finite, closed-form sum over ordered $p$-values $p_{(1)} < \dots < p_{(J)}$ (with $p_{(J+1)} = u_p$):

$$
\sum_{j=1}^{J}(p_{(j+1)} - p_{(j)})\,\hat{\mathbb{P}}(p_{(j)}, C)^2 - \frac{1}{u_p N}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\xi_j^{C}\big(u_p^2 - p_{(j)}^2\big) + \frac{u_p}{3},
$$

where $\xi_j^{C}$ counts window results at $p_{(j)}$ with $\mathrm{eJAB}_{01} > C$; the minimiser is found by grid search on $C \in [0, 3]$.

> **Detection rule.** Flag study $i$ as likely not to replicate if $\;p_i \leq \alpha\;$ and $\;\mathrm{eJAB}_{01,i} > \hat C^{*}$.

### Diagnostics

After flagging we check whether the flagged set behaves as a genuine collection of non-replications should. By Derivation 1 each candidate sits in $p_i \in (f_i, \alpha]$; under left-tail uniformity, conditioning on $\mathcal{H}_0$ gives $p_i \mid \mathcal{H}_0, p_i \in (f_i, \alpha] \sim \mathrm{Unif}(f_i, \alpha]$. Affinely rescale to the unit interval, then push through the standard-normal quantile $\Phi^{-1}$:

$$
U_i = \frac{p_i - f_i}{\alpha - f_i} \sim \mathrm{Unif}(0,1), \qquad Z_i = \Phi^{-1}(U_i) \sim N(0,1).
$$

The $Z$ scale reads directly: the normal QQ-plot is a straight line, and an index plot of $Z_i$ carries flat $\pm 2$ bands. Candidates with $|Z_i| > 2$ are flagged for review as unlikely to be true non-replications, and may be false positives.

## 2. Implementation

The method ships as two tools: an **R package** for scripted workflows and a **JASP module** for point-and-click use. Both take one row per hypothesis test (columns $p$, $n$, $q$, study ID) and share the defaults $\alpha = 0.05$, $u_p = 0.10$.

### R package: `ejabT1E`

A small set of base-R functions (only `stats` imported) implementing the calibrated detector and its diagnostics. The high-level entry point `ejab_pipeline()` returns $\hat C^{*}$, the objective value, the candidate rows, the diagnostic $U_i$ values, and all $\mathrm{eJAB}_{01}$ values.

- `ejab01(p, n, q)` — the $\mathrm{eJAB}_{01}$ Bayes factor.
- `estimate_Cstar(p, ejab, up)` — grid search for $\hat C^{*}$.
- `detect_type1(p, ejab, alpha, Cstar)` — flag candidates.
- `diagnostic_U(p, n, q, alpha, Cstar)` — the QQ / $Z$ diagnostics.
- `ejab_pipeline(df, up, alpha)` — the full pipeline.

```r
library(ejabT1E)
res <- ejab_pipeline(df, up = 0.10, alpha = 0.05)
res$Cstar       # calibrated threshold
res$candidates  # flagged non-replications
```

Lower-level functions compose the steps explicitly and reproduce the pipeline output exactly; $\hat C^{*}$ is obtained by closed-form evaluation of the objective from Derivation 2.

### JASP module: Ahab's Harpoon

[JASP](https://jasp-stats.org) is a free, open-source point-and-click statistics platform widely used in psychology. The **Ahab's Harpoon** module exposes the full detector as a single *eJAB Analysis*: assign the $p$, $n$, $q$, and study-ID columns, set $\alpha$ and $u_p$, and the module produces

- a **summary table** ($\hat C^{*}$, objective value, candidates flagged, valid rows) and a **candidates table** ($\mathrm{eJAB}_{01}$ for each flagged result);
- a **calibration plot**: contradiction rate against $\alpha$ versus the target $\alpha / u_p$;
- the two **$Z$-diagnostic plots**: a normal QQ-plot of $Z$ and an index plot of $Z$ with $\pm 2$ bands.

The RPP and RPCB datasets ship bundled as worked examples.

## 3. Simulation studies

We validate the calibrated detector on synthetic corpora where the null/non-null label of each test is known. For a target true-null proportion $\pi_0$ and effect-size distribution on the non-nulls, we draw $(p_i, n_i, q_i)$ jointly, compute $\hat C^{*}$ on the simulated corpus, and score the flagging rule against ground truth. We sweep $\pi_0$, the effect-size scale, and $(n, q)$ ranges across replicates.

## 4. Application

As a test bed we use three large replication corpora, spanning psychology (RPP), preclinical cancer biology (RPCB), and the social and behavioural sciences (SCORE / DARPA). Each pairs original findings with independent replication outcomes, so the method can be scored against ground truth via confusion matrices. The decision rule is the *locked default*, not retuned per corpus; metrics use the significant subset ($p < \alpha$), with "did not replicate" as the positive class. In the matrices below, **red cells are errors** and light cells are correct decisions.
